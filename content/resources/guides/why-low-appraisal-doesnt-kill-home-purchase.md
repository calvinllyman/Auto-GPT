One of the most stressful moments in a real estate transaction is hearing the words:

*"The appraisal came in low."*

Many buyers immediately assume the deal is over, while many sellers worry they'll have no choice but to slash their asking price. In reality, a low appraisal is often just another hurdle to work through — not necessarily the end of the transaction.

I've seen firsthand how emotionally draining this situation can be. Buyers have already imagined themselves living in the home, sellers have begun planning their move, and suddenly everyone is questioning whether the deal will survive.

The good news is that a low appraisal doesn't automatically determine the outcome.

## What Is an Appraisal?

An appraisal is an independent opinion of a property's market value prepared by a licensed appraiser. Lenders use the appraisal to ensure they're not lending substantially more than the property's estimated value.

It's important to understand that an appraisal is **not** the same thing as:

- A home inspection
- A comparative market analysis (CMA) prepared by a Realtor
- The price a buyer and seller agreed upon

Each serves a different purpose.

## Why Do Appraisals Come in Low?

There are several reasons this can happen.

### Comparable sales may be limited

Appraisers rely heavily on recently sold homes that are similar in size, age, condition, and location.

In neighborhoods with very few recent sales — or where homes are unique — finding strong comparable properties can be difficult. That can show up in Yukon, Mustang, Oklahoma City, and other metro pockets where inventory or uniqueness makes clean comps harder to find.

### Rapidly changing markets

When home values are rising quickly, closed sales may lag behind the current market.

Buyers may legitimately be willing to pay more than the historical sales data reflects.

### Unique improvements

Some upgrades simply don't translate dollar-for-dollar into appraised value.

Examples include:

- Custom finishes
- Premium landscaping
- Smart home technology
- Certain energy-efficient improvements
- Specialized workshops or hobby spaces

Just because an owner spent money doesn't necessarily mean the market recognizes that full amount in value.

### Important details may be missing

Sometimes relevant comparable sales weren't available when the appraisal was completed.

Occasionally an appraiser may overlook upgrades, recent renovations, or neighborhood trends that deserve additional consideration.

## What Happens If the Appraisal Is Lower Than the Contract Price?

Every transaction is different, but there are several possible outcomes.

### The seller lowers the price

This is often the simplest solution when both parties want to move forward.

### The buyer brings additional cash

Some buyers choose to pay the difference between the appraised value and the purchase price.

Whether this makes financial sense depends on the buyer's long-term goals and financial situation.

### Buyer and seller negotiate

Frequently, both sides make concessions.

The purchase price may change, repairs may be renegotiated, closing costs may be adjusted, or other creative solutions may help bridge the gap.

### The appraisal is reconsidered

If there is credible evidence that important comparable sales or relevant market information were overlooked, the lender may accept a **Reconsideration of Value (ROV)** request.

An ROV isn't simply an opportunity to argue with the appraiser. It should present factual information, such as:

- Better comparable sales
- Incorrect measurements or property details
- Significant upgrades that weren't considered
- Other objective market evidence

While not every reconsideration results in a value change, a well-supported request can sometimes make a meaningful difference.

### A new appraisal may be possible

In some circumstances — depending on the loan type, lender policies, and the specific situation — a different appraisal process may become available. This isn't always an option, but it's worth discussing with your lender and Realtor before assuming the transaction cannot move forward.

## Don't Let Emotion Drive the Decision

When an appraisal comes in lower than expected, emotions often run high.

Buyers may feel like they're overpaying.

Sellers may feel like their home has been unfairly undervalued.

The reality is that neither reaction necessarily reflects the true market.

An appraisal is one professional opinion based on available market data at a specific point in time. It is an important part of the lending process, but it doesn't always tell the entire story.

## Why Having the Right Realtor Matters

This is where experienced representation becomes especially valuable.

A good Realtor doesn't simply accept a low appraisal and hope for the best.

Instead, they should:

- Carefully review the appraisal for factual errors
- Compare the appraiser's selected comparable sales with other recent market activity
- Work with the lender when appropriate to provide additional market data
- Help both parties understand their available options
- Negotiate creative solutions that keep the transaction moving whenever possible

Not every low appraisal can be overcome. Sometimes the numbers simply don't support the contract price.

However, many transactions successfully close because the professionals involved remain calm, communicate effectively, and focus on solving the problem rather than assigning blame.

## Final Thoughts

If you're buying or selling a home and receive disappointing appraisal news, don't assume the deal is over.

Take a step back.

Review the facts.

Understand your options.

Then work with professionals who can help you navigate the process with clear communication and thoughtful negotiation.

A low appraisal may change the path to closing, but it doesn't always have to change the destination.
