A competitive offer is the package a seller believes will actually close. The highest number on page one is not always the winning strategy — especially if financing, timelines, or inspection posture create doubt.

## What sellers usually weigh

- Price vs. recent comparable sales
- Earnest money amount and timing
- Strength of financing (or proof of funds)
- Option period length and inspection posture
- Closing date and possession needs
- Contingencies and special requests
- Cleanliness of the paperwork and communication

## Price with comps, not adrenaline

Ask for sold comps that match the home’s size, condition, and location. Bidding far above weak comps can create appraisal risk. Bidding far below a well-priced home in a busy pocket can end the conversation immediately.

If appraisal risk is real, understand your options before you write: [low appraisal guide](/resources/guides/why-low-appraisal-doesnt-kill-home-purchase).

## Terms that often matter as much as price

### Earnest money

Signals seriousness. Size it appropriately for the price point and your comfort. Learn the basics in [What is earnest money?](/resources/faqs/what-is-earnest-money).

### Option period

Enough time to inspect — not so long that the seller feels parked. Details: [Oklahoma option period explained](/resources/guides/oklahoma-option-period-explained).

### Closing date

Align lender capability, your lease end, and the seller’s move. A “faster close” only helps if it is real.

### Appraisal and financing language

Know what you are promising. Appraisal-gap language means cash risk — never add it casually.

### Seller credits

Useful when payment or cash-to-close matters more than sticker price. Confirm what your loan program allows. See [Closing costs explained](/resources/guides/closing-costs-explained).

## Escalation clauses and love letters

Tools and tactics change with market conditions and local norms. What matters permanently is clarity: do not invent a structure you do not understand. Ask before you use clever language that creates unintended obligations.

## Emotional discipline

Tour with a written must-have list. Sleep on it when you can. Then write with a plan. Use the [offer review checklist](/resources/checklists/offer-review-checklist) before you send anything.
