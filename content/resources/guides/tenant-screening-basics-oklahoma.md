Good tenants are not found by luck. They are selected with clear criteria, consistent process, and documentation. Screening poorly is how vacancy problems turn into eviction problems.

This is general education — not legal advice. Fair housing rules matter; apply standards evenly to every applicant.

## Decide your criteria before you advertise

Write down what you will require, such as:

- Income-to-rent guideline (and how you verify income)
- Acceptable credit / background considerations you will actually use
- Rental history expectations
- Pet policy (or no pets) stated clearly
- Occupancy limits consistent with the property and local rules

If you change standards mid-stream for one applicant, you create risk and confusion.

## What to collect

- Application for each adult
- Photo ID
- Income documentation
- Authorization for screening (as required by your process/provider)
- Pet information if applicable
- References (prior landlords when available)

Use the same packet every time.

## How to read an application without overreacting

Look for patterns:

- Stable income that supports the rent
- Consistent address history vs. unexplained gaps
- Prior landlord references that confirm on-time pay and property care
- Transparency about issues (a clear explanation can be better than a hidden surprise)

One imperfect metric does not automatically mean “no” — but multiple weak signals usually do.

## Red flags that deserve a pause

- Pressure to skip screening “because I’m perfect”
- Unwillingness to document income
- Inconsistent stories about employment or prior addresses
- Requests to pay outside normal documented channels
- Hostility when asked routine questions

## Approvals, denials, and communication

- Decide promptly once information is complete
- Communicate clearly and professionally
- Keep records of what you received and when you decided
- If you deny based on screening reports, follow any required adverse-action processes that apply to your situation

## After you approve

A strong screening process still needs a strong lease:

- Clear rent, fees, and due dates
- Maintenance responsibilities
- Pet and guest rules
- Entry notice and showing rules if you may sell later

Then make move-in documentation part of the handoff — encourage tenants to use a thorough [move-in checklist](/resources/checklists/tenant-move-in-checklist) mindset even if you provide your own form.
